The Zen of Hanzo

64 patterns for exploration, progress, and continuous improvement.

The I Ching describes change through 64 hexagrams—patterns of emergence, discovery, resistance, cooperation, transformation, and renewal.At Hanzo, we use these patterns as inspiration for building intelligent systems and advancing research.Science progresses by asking questions, challenging assumptions, testing ideas, learning from failure, and discovering what becomes possible next.Intelligence grows through that same process.Eight categories. Sixty-four perspectives. Infinite possibilities for discovery.

Explore the patterns

These are modern Hanzo interpretations inspired by the I Ching, not claims about the text’s historical or scientific meaning. The eight categories are Hanzo’s own editorial grouping of the hexagrams in King Wen order.

I — Explore

01–08 · Curiosity, possibility, and the beginning of discovery

Every breakthrough begins with a question. Exploration requires imagination, openness, uncertainty, and the willingness to venture beyond what is already understood.

  • 01

    The Creative — Origination

    Inventor · Generate new possibilities. Imagine what does not yet exist, then seek a way to test it.

  • 02

    The Receptive — Observation

    Empiricist · Receive evidence without forcing it to fit existing expectations.

  • 03

    Difficulty at the Beginning — Emergence

    Pioneer · Begin productive work amid uncertainty. Establish the first usable foundations.

  • 04

    Youthful Folly — Inquiry

    Learner · Recognize what is unknown. Ask questions before assuming answers.

  • 05

    Waiting — Preparation

    Strategist · Gather evidence, establish readiness, and recognize when conditions support action.

  • 06

    Conflict — Critical Debate

    Skeptic · Investigate competing explanations. Use disagreement to reveal hidden assumptions.

  • 07

    The Army — Coordination

    Research Lead · Organize people, agents, knowledge, and resources around a shared objective.

  • 08

    Holding Together — Collaboration

    Collaborator · Connect complementary capabilities and align participants around common evidence.

II — Form

09–16 · Structure, methodology, and collective capability

Ideas become useful when they acquire structure. Formation transforms possibilities into methods, systems, experiments, and coordinated research programs.

  • 09

    Small Taming — Incremental Progress

    Iterative Builder · Make small, measurable improvements before attempting larger changes.

  • 10

    Treading — Methodological Care

    Methodologist · Proceed with discipline, understand the risks, and respect experimental boundaries.

  • 11

    Peace — Alignment

    Systems Architect · Align objectives, methods, resources, and interfaces so progress can flow.

  • 12

    Standstill — Reassessment

    Diagnostician · Recognize stagnation. Identify what prevents learning or execution.

  • 13

    Fellowship — Open Cooperation

    Community Builder · Advance knowledge through shared methods, open inquiry, and collaboration.

  • 14

    Great Possession — Stewardship

    Research Steward · Manage abundant knowledge, compute, talent, and resources responsibly.

  • 15

    Modesty — Intellectual Humility

    Calibrator · Distinguish confidence from certainty. Remain willing to revise conclusions.

  • 16

    Enthusiasm — Mobilization

    Program Director · Convert promising ideas into coordinated initiatives with clear goals.

III — Observe

17–24 · Evidence, measurement, understanding, and correction

Progress depends on the ability to see reality clearly. Observation connects hypotheses to evidence and makes errors visible before they become embedded in systems.

  • 17

    Following — Responsiveness

    Adaptive Researcher · Follow reliable evidence when it leads somewhere unexpected.

  • 18

    Work on the Decayed — Correction

    Research Maintainer · Identify outdated assumptions, faulty methods, and accumulated technical debt.

  • 19

    Approach — Accessibility

    Knowledge Translator · Make complex systems, tools, and findings understandable and usable.

  • 20

    Contemplation — Observability

    Scientific Observer · Study behavior carefully before attempting to change it.

  • 21

    Biting Through — Rigorous Testing

    Verifier · Confront inconsistencies directly. Resolve questions with explicit tests and evidence.

  • 22

    Grace — Representation

    Scientific Communicator · Make discoveries legible through clear models, figures, interfaces, and explanations.

  • 23

    Splitting Apart — Decomposition

    Analyst · Break complex problems into components that can be independently examined.

  • 24

    Return — Reproducibility

    Replication Scientist · Return to reference conditions, repeat experiments, and establish reliable baselines.

IV — Experiment

25–32 · Testing, computation, uncertainty, and endurance

Experiments transform possibilities into evidence. Robust research requires trustworthy inputs, controlled execution, transparent measurements, and resilience when reality differs from prediction.

  • 25

    Innocence — Unbiased Investigation

    Experimental Designer · Test hypotheses without manipulating the conditions to favor an expected outcome.

  • 26

    Great Taming — Accumulated Capability

    Infrastructure Builder · Develop the instruments, models, datasets, and computational capacity needed for difficult investigations.

  • 27

    Nourishment — Input Quality

    Data Curator · Recognize that the reliability of results depends on the quality of evidence, data, and inputs.

  • 28

    Great Exceeding — Stress Testing

    Reliability Engineer · Explore extreme conditions and discover where existing methods fail.

  • 29

    The Abyss — Resilience

    Failure Researcher · Investigate uncertainty, repeated failures, and adverse conditions without losing the research record.

  • 30

    Clinging Fire — Transparency

    Measurement Scientist · Illuminate internal behavior through instrumentation, diagnostics, and interpretable measurements.

  • 31

    Influence — Interaction

    Systems Scientist · Investigate relationships, feedback, dependencies, and possible causal mechanisms.

  • 32

    Duration — Longitudinal Validity

    Longitudinal Researcher · Test whether findings remain reliable across time, environments, and changing conditions.

V — Adapt

33–40 · Constraints, resistance, changing conditions, and new directions

Research rarely proceeds exactly as planned. Adaptation is the ability to recognize limitations, reconsider assumptions, and change direction without losing what has already been learned.

  • 33

    Retreat — Strategic Withdrawal

    Research Strategist · Stop unproductive investigations when evidence no longer justifies their cost.

  • 34

    Great Power — Controlled Capability

    Safety Engineer · Apply powerful tools with appropriate limits, safeguards, and oversight.

  • 35

    Progress — Measured Advancement

    Performance Researcher · Establish whether a new method represents a genuine improvement over its baseline.

  • 36

    Darkening of the Light — Protected Knowledge

    Research Security Steward · Protect sensitive discoveries, research participants, confidential data, and intellectual property.

  • 37

    The Family — Local Responsibility

    Team Architect · Establish clear ownership, trusted collaboration, and accountable research groups.

  • 38

    Opposition — Diverse Perspectives

    Contrarian Researcher · Explore alternative models, contradictory observations, and competing theories.

  • 39

    Obstruction — Constraint Discovery

    Bottleneck Investigator · Identify the physical, computational, organizational, or methodological limitation preventing progress.

  • 40

    Deliverance — Unblocking

    Problem Solver · Remove unnecessary constraints and restore the ability to investigate and experiment.

VI — Optimize

41–48 · Efficiency, leverage, experimental value, and shared foundations

Research resources are finite. Optimization seeks better results through intelligent allocation of time, compute, energy, capital, instruments, and human attention.

  • 41

    Decrease — Simplification

    Minimalist · Remove unnecessary complexity while preserving essential scientific capability.

  • 42

    Increase — Leverage

    Optimization Scientist · Identify improvements that amplify productivity across many experiments or systems.

  • 43

    Breakthrough — Evidence-Based Decision

    Decision Scientist · Make consequential choices when evidence satisfies defined criteria.

  • 44

    Coming to Meet — Opportunity Assessment

    Technology Scout · Evaluate unexpected discoveries, emerging methods, and external innovations before integrating them.

  • 45

    Gathering Together — Knowledge Integration

    Knowledge Engineer · Bring complementary datasets, tools, expertise, and discoveries into a coherent research system.

  • 46

    Pushing Upward — Scalable Progress

    Scaling Researcher · Extend validated methods to larger problems without losing correctness or efficiency.

  • 47

    Oppression — Resource Constraints

    Resource Optimizer · Continue useful investigation under limited compute, funding, time, or experimental capacity.

  • 48

    The Well — Shared Infrastructure

    Platform Engineer · Build reusable scientific foundations that support many researchers and disciplines.

VII — Transform

49–56 · New methods, synthesis, deployment, and transformation

Discovery changes what is possible. Transformation converts established knowledge into new architectures, research methods, technologies, and practical capabilities.

  • 49

    Revolution — Paradigm Change

    Scientific Revolutionary · Replace an established approach when stronger evidence supports a fundamentally better alternative.

  • 50

    The Cauldron — Synthesis

    Interdisciplinary Inventor · Combine methods and insights from different disciplines to create new capabilities.

  • 51

    Thunder — Discovery Response

    Rapid Investigator · Respond constructively to unexpected findings, anomalies, or sudden changes.

  • 52

    Keeping Still — Deliberate Restraint

    Research Guardian · Pause when additional investigation, independent review, or stronger controls are required.

  • 53

    Development — Cumulative Discovery

    Long-Horizon Researcher · Build understanding through successive refinements and validated milestones.

  • 54

    The Marrying Maiden — Integration Risk

    Integration Scientist · Examine compatibility, hidden dependencies, and unintended consequences when combining systems.

  • 55

    Abundance — Signal Selection

    Scientific Synthesist · Extract meaningful findings from abundant observations, experiments, and possibilities.

  • 56

    The Wanderer — Portability

    Transfer Scientist · Move methods and knowledge between laboratories, disciplines, hardware, and organizations.

VIII — Evolve

57–64 · Dissemination, verification, maturity, and continuous becoming

Every result changes the conditions for future research. Evolution turns discoveries into shared knowledge, mature capabilities, and new questions.

  • 57

    The Gentle Wind — Composability

    Scientific Toolsmith · Spread useful capabilities through reusable, interoperable methods and components.

  • 58

    The Joyous Lake — Knowledge Exchange

    Scientific Collaborator · Advance understanding through reciprocal learning, constructive criticism, and open dialogue.

  • 59

    Dispersion — Distributed Discovery

    Research Network Architect · Enable independent researchers and institutions to contribute to a larger body of knowledge.

  • 60

    Limitation — Productive Constraints

    Constraint Designer · Use explicit limits and standards to make research safer, clearer, and more reproducible.

  • 61

    Inner Truth — Verifiability

    Evidence Auditor · Ground claims in traceable observations, documented methods, and reproducible analysis.

  • 62

    Small Exceeding — Precision

    Precision Engineer · Refine small details whose effects can determine scientific or engineering success.

  • 63

    After Completion — Operational Maturity

    Release Steward · Preserve reliability through validation, monitoring, maintenance, and disciplined change.

  • 64

    Before Completion — Continuous Becoming

    Lifelong Explorer · Recognize that every answer creates new questions and every achievement opens new possibilities.

Intelligence is never finished.

A model can become more capable. An experiment can reveal a better question. A failed hypothesis can redirect an entire research program. A discovery in one discipline can transform another.

Progress is not a straight line, and intelligence is not a fixed destination.

These 64 patterns provide different perspectives for recognizing the condition of a problem and choosing how to proceed.

The same principles can inform the development of an AI model, a scientific experiment, a research laboratory, an organization, or an evolving technological ecosystem.

Explore. Form. Observe. Experiment. Adapt. Optimize. Transform. Evolve.

And begin again.
