Planning range

Qualification. Four 8-GPU servers is where a qualification starts.

Isolated production pools. One pool for each specialist, so a busy class of work never takes memory from another.

Redundant pools. Two independent replicas of those pools across failure domains.

Around the servers

NVLink and NVSwitch inside each server.

400 or 800 Gb/s RDMA wherever a specialist spans servers.

About 31 TB of NVMe in each server (8 x 3.84 TB).

Separate sandbox workers for coding agents.

A high-availability control cluster.

Final capacity and performance commitments come from qualifying your workload: context length, concurrency, routing mix, latency, and failover.

Questions

How is Enterprise priced?
Each contract is priced for your organization: where Hanzo runs and the compute you need. Members are unlimited. Talk to sales for a quote.
Where do the savings figures come from?
Every rate is read from our price list and our plan list when the page opens; the volumes, the people and the GPUs are yours. The figure for your own GPUs counts them on all month at an hourly rate and leaves out the Enterprise agreement, which our team prices with you.
Can Hanzo run fully offline?
Yes. Hanzo runs air-gapped on your own servers, with models served on machines you control.
How is our data used?
Your data is used to run your service. Training Hanzo models on it takes your administrator's choice or a written agreement. Read the Privacy Policy.
Which identity providers work?
Your own OpenID Connect provider, for single sign-on. SCIM 2.0 creates, updates and removes accounts as your directory changes.
Can we start on Team and move up?
Yes. Team covers 2 to 100 people today, with single sign-on, roles and an audit log. Move to Enterprise when you want your own infrastructure, dedicated compute or more people. Compare plans.
