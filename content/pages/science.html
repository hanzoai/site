01One scientific execution engine across every research-driven industry
Scientific and engineering breakthroughs are slowed by the gaps between literature, data, code, simulators, instruments, compute clusters, and peer review. Hanzo Science unifies the six-stage research loop so every hypothesis, simulation, figure, and decision stays linked to the exact code, container digest, and evidence that produced it.
The 6-stage universal research loop — backed by a branching Research GraphBranch competing hypotheses · Run in parallel · Preserve negative results
01 · DiscoverReview knowledge, identify gaps & generate ideasIngest papers, patents, ELN/LIMS entries, astronomical catalogs, and prior negative findings with direct DOI/PMID/arXiv provenance.
02 · DesignForm competing hypotheses, select methods & planBranch competing hypotheses in a research graph, define baselines, controls, simulation matrices, and pre-registered acceptance thresholds.
03 · ExecuteRun code, train models, simulate, test or measureDispatch reproducible containers, live Hanzo Notebook kernels, OpenMDAO / Phonopy solvers, targets + crew DAGs, and Slurm/cloud GPU sweeps.
04 · EvaluateAnalyze evidence, uncertainty, failures & resultsAudit statistical validity, conservation laws, numerical equivalence, cycle hysteresis, calibration error, and assertion-level source support.
05 · OptimizeCompare alternatives, refine methods & allocate budgetMaximize validated information gained per unit of compute using Bayesian optimization, active learning, and Pareto frontier search.
06 · CommunicateReproduce findings, review, publish or transferCompile LaTeX manuscripts, CDISC ADaM/rtables TLFs, qualified model cards, and RO-Crate / W3C PROV-O lineage bundles.
Supports both fundamental discovery and applied R&D
Fundamental DiscoveryExploratory researchGenerate new questions, develop theories, investigate unexpected observations, and branch into competing explanations when there is no predetermined optimization target.Example: What new attention or state-space architectures might overcome long-context efficiency limitations, or which cellular mechanisms drive resistance in a cohort?First-class support for mathematical proofs (Lean 4), theoretical modeling, systematic literature synthesis, observational studies, and exploratory simulations.
Applied R&D & EngineeringGoal-directed researchOptimize a defined outcome, compare candidate designs, test a specific hypothesis, and solve a concrete engineering or clinical problem under resource bounds.Example: Reduce Kai high-cardinality inference latency by 35% while preserving numerical equivalence and calibration, or maximize rocket nozzle Isp within wall thermal limits.First-class support for controlled ablations, multi-objective Pareto sweeps, OpenMDAO multidisciplinary design optimization, and release qualification gates.

1 Problem statementComparative single-cell transcriptomics asks whether a cell type in one species has a homolog in another, and how its expression program has been conserved or rewired. The technical obstacle is that any two species’ transcriptomes live in different gene coordinate systems.
L(θ, φ) = E_q[log p_θ(x | z, s)] - D_KL(q_φ(z | x, s) || p(z)) (1)M_ij = ⟨x_i, H y_j⟩ / (||x_i|| ||H y_j||) (2)
SAMap [10] replaced the hard ortholog filter with a soft, BLAST-derived gene-homology weight matrix H and iteratively refined it against cell-level manifold alignment. SATURN [11] and UCE [12] replace H entirely with ESM2 protein-language-model embeddings in a shared 1,280-dimensional protein space.2 Ortholog-subsetting methodsSeurat v3 [1] (PMID 31178118) finds canonical-correlation vectors u, v maximising corr(u^T X, v^T Y) over shared-ortholog matrices, while LIGER [2] (PMID 31178122) factorises each dataset via integrative NMF X_k ≈ (W + V_k) H_k.

02 · Claim supportDoes the cited passage or table actually support the assertion?Assertion-level extraction comparing the manuscript sentence against the primary source table, sample size, and experimental conditions.
03 · Computational integrityDid the code execute cleanly and produce the reported numbers?Direct kernel execution check confirming zero silent NaNs, non-zero exit codes, or mismatches between figure annotations and output tensors.
04 · ReproducibilityCan another scientist re-run the exact computation?Records OCI container digest, conda/lockfile hashes, random seeds, input dataset SHA-256, hardware topology, and RO-Crate bundle.
05 · Method validityAre controls, baselines, units, and physical laws respected?Domain-specific checks: acoustic sum rule (ASR) at Γ for phonons, cycle closure hysteresis for FEP, PCA/Harmony baselines for scVI, and STA corners for RTL.
06 · Scientific uncertaintyAre error bars, sensitivity bounds, and alternative hypotheses reported?Requires confidence/credible intervals, convergence diagnostics (Rhat, ESS), and sensitivity sweeps across key hyperparameters.
07 · Domain reviewWhich decisions require accountable human sign-off?Routes synthesis nominations, clinical TLF locks, silicon tapeout checklists, and model releases to named human reviewers via Kai policy gates.

Scientific review often turns on a small detail. Suppose a veterinary study reports that missed appointments fell from 12% to 9% after clinics introduced reminders. That’s a drop of three percentage points, or 25% relative to the original rate. Both figures are correct. Whether reminders caused the decline is a separate question, and the answer depends on how the study was designed.

Hanzo Science helps researchers keep those distinctions visible as they work.

Read across the literature and your own records

Start with a question, a collection of papers, or an existing dataset. Hanzo searches the public web alongside the articles, notes, and datasets your team adds. It can find an exact phrase or related work that uses different terminology, then cite the material behind its answer.

For a team studying appointment reminders, that could mean finding papers that describe missed visits as nonattendance, pulling out how each study defined a missed visit, and identifying which studies used a comparison group. A colleague can open the cited source and check whether the studies belong in the same analysis.

Run the analysis and keep the evidence

Use a Hanzo sandbox to run a calculation, or a GPU machine for work that needs more compute. Keep the output alongside the question it answers. If two clinics counted cancellations differently, a researcher can rerun the comparison using a common definition and see how much the finding changes.

Hanzo also records when evidence entered the research record. When a new paper changes the team’s conclusion, researchers can revisit what they knew at the time of the original decision and identify the evidence that changed their minds.

Test the research method itself

Teams can use Hanzo Evals to compare prompts or models against the same set of expert-reviewed questions. A lab might score three things separately: whether the system found the right paper, whether the cited passage supports the claim, and whether the calculation is correct. That makes it easier to see exactly where a revised method improves the work and where it still needs expert judgment.

Get started

Bring a question your team has already answered, the sources you trusted, and the result you accepted. Run that question through Hanzo Science and have a colleague review the evidence. A useful first test is whether that colleague can explain how the team reached its conclusion and reproduce the calculation behind it.
